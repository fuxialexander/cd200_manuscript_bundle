Chromosome-held-out accessibility prediction across three datasets. (a) Pearson correlation between predicted and observed ln(1 + total counts) in held-out peak windows. (b) Mean per-window Jensen–Shannon (JS) distance between predicted and observed 1-bp profile distributions in held-out peak windows; smaller values indicate better agreement. JS distance is the square root of natural-log JS divergence. (c) Count correlation across the complete held-out evaluation sets, including peaks and sampled background regions. All outputs cover the same central 1,000 bp of a 2,114-bp sequence input. Points are checkpoint point estimates; bars are 95% percentile confidence intervals from 2,000 paired chromosome-stratified genomic-block bootstrap replicates (nominal 1-Mb blocks merged where input windows overlap; bootstrap seed 1729). Intervals quantify sampling uncertainty conditional on each trained checkpoint, not variation across training runs or experimental replicates. Newly trained models use one predeclared split per dataset and one training seed (1234). Published human ChromBPNet checkpoints are evaluated as released; they are not additional independently trained repeats. Native ExplaiNN predicts scalar counts and has no profile output; its missing profile points are N/A, not zero. No published mouse ChromBPNet comparator is included.

The human held-out chromosomes are chr1, chr3 and chr6; the mouse held-out chromosomes are chr16, chr8 and chr9. Peak-window counts are 62,929 (GM12878), 66,474 (K562) and 13,593 (mouse cSCC); complete evaluation-set sizes are 314,645, 332,370 and 26,350, respectively. Human targets are insertion counts; mouse targets are unsmoothed per-base ATAC counts from the bundled Zarr store, for which original Tn5 endpoint/shift provenance has not been independently established. Absolute JS distances are therefore not interpreted as a cross-dataset ranking. These are fixed held-out region sets, not an exhaustive base-by-base genome scan.

MoDL has the highest peak count correlation and lowest peak profile JS distance among the evaluated models in all three datasets. The complete-region count comparison favors ChromBPNet in the two human benchmarks. In mouse, its count-correlation point estimate is slightly higher, but the paired 95% confidence interval for the difference includes zero. Model comparisons should use the paired difference intervals in the source evaluation files rather than infer significance from overlap of the individual intervals shown here.
